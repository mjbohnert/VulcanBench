The Voice Tax (what a model loses when it hears the question instead of reading it)

Two hundred held-out questions delivered twice — typed, and spoken via TTS under three voices, 1.25x speech, and 10 dB ambient noise — scored by one modality-blind scorer
Both flagship voice models lose real accuracy when the question is spoken instead of typed — but not much, and not evenly. Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0 leads on text (99.0%), on audio (95.7%), and pays the smaller tax (+3.3 vs +4.0 pp). The tax concentrates in spoken arithmetic (~10 pp for both models); on numeric extraction, Grok’s goes negative.

1. The tax lives in arithmetic: both models drop ~10 pp on spoken mental math — operands you can’t re-read. Every other category taxes ≤6 pp, and Grok is slightly better at extracting numbers from speech than text (−1.7 pp).

2. Latency profiles are opposites: GPT Realtime speaks first (0.53 s vs 1.22 s to first token) but Grok finishes first (1.43 s vs 2.70 s per turn) — its reason-while-speaking design in one row of numbers.

3. The models disagree on which voice is hard (Grok hears fable best; GPT’s worst is shimmer), and 10 dB of noise costs Grok 5 pp, GPT nothing (n=60).

Method. Voice Eval Suite v1: 200 original held-out questions (never from public benchmarks), TTS-rendered (tts-1: onyx, shimmer, fable), one attempt per unit, one modality-blind scorer (normalize → exact/alias → judge anthropic:claude-opus-5), text baselines through the same realtime sessions as audio. Caveats: single attempts (±1 item ≈ 0.5 pp); fast/noise on the n=60 seeded subset; first-token here is first transcript delta — not comparable to xAI’s advertised time-to-first-audio.
