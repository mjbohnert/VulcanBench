Claude Opus 5 (low vs. medium vs. high effort)

Twenty-three real merged PRs across Python, Rust, TypeScript, JavaScript, and Go, graded by deterministic hidden tests
The cheapest setting wins: 20/23 (87.0%) at $0.70 per solved task. Raising effort triples the spend and solves two fewer tasks. High returns the fewest wrong answers of any configuration — its deficit is unfinished work, four runs cut off by the wall-clock budget — so even granting it unlimited time, its ceiling is a tie.

1. No configuration beats low on accuracy, and none approaches it on economics: $0.70 per solved task against $1.27 and $2.42. Effort buys exactly one task — itertools-strip-prefix, wrong at low and medium, solved at high — for an extra $29.53.

2. High’s losses are not mistakes. It returns one wrong answer to low’s three, then times out on the largest repositories: aiohttp at 45 minutes and NetworkX-Leiden at 60, both solved at low in under 12. Its ceiling here is partly the clock, not the model. PennyLane Trotter, unsolved by every configuration ever run, takes its first non-zero score at low effort (0.40) and runs the full hour for nothing at medium and high.
