July 26, 2026 | v3 suite | 23 tasks | 69 runs | 3 effort levels | 5 languages | $81.76

Claude Opus 5 across the effort knob

First measurement of Opus 5 on the full v3 suite: twenty-three real merged post-cutoff PRs across Python, Rust, TypeScript, JavaScript, and Go, graded by deterministic hidden tests, at low, medium, and high effort.

Abstract

Claude Opus 5’s cheapest setting is its best: 20/23 (87.0%) at $0.70 per solved task on low effort. Score falls at every step up the knob — 20 → 19 → 18 — while suite cost triples ($14.07 → $43.60). The deficit is unfinished work, not bad work: high effort returns the fewest wrong answers of any setting (one, against three at low) and the most wall-clock timeouts (four). Two of its three regressions are budget cutoffs on tasks it solves at low, so granting it unlimited time its ceiling is a tie, at 3.1× the cost. The sweep also set the first non-zero score ever recorded on PennyLane Trotter fragmentation, and surfaced a harness bug with consequences beyond this report.

Findings

1. Low is never worse than high, and costs a third as much. Measured, low leads 20 to 18. Two of high’s three regressions are budget cutoffs on tasks it solves at low — grant it unlimited time on both and it reaches 20/23, a tie, at 3.1× the cost. There is no reading of this sweep where paying for effort wins.

2. Effort trades wrong answers for unfinished runs. Wrong answers fall (3 → 3 → 1); runs that never finish climb (0 → 1 → 4). High spends 3× the tokens and 2.6× the clock per task, then times out on the biggest repositories — networkx-leiden cut off at 60 minutes after solving in 11.6 at low, aiohttp at 45 after solving in 3.9. The one regression the clock does not explain is flask-teardown-robust: it finished, and returned a worse patch (0.67) than at low.

3. Effort buys exactly one task. itertools-strip-prefix is a genuine wrong answer at low and medium and solved at high — the same task GPT-5.6 Sol went 0-for-3 on in Report 07. One task, for an extra $29.53 of suite spend.

4. First crack in a wall. pennylane-trotter-fragmented has never been solved by any model on v3. At low effort Opus 5 finishes it with a 0.40 partial — the first non-zero score recorded on it; at medium and high it runs the full 60 minutes and scores nothing. Report 07’s effort-discriminator inverts too: flask-teardown-robust, which all three earlier models needed high effort to solve, falls to Opus 5 at low.

The 16 K output cap

A first pass at the high column returned 17/23. It was an artifact: five of six unfinished runs died on a hardcoded 16,000-token output cap in the harness (harness/agent/providers.py), returning no content and no tool calls. Thinking bills against max_tokens, so raising effort raised the truncation rate — and the harness scored the empty patch as a failed attempt, indistinguishable in the summary from a model that tried and produced a wrong answer.

Fixed two ways: max_tokens now scales with effort (32 K at low through 128 K at max, the model ceiling), and a truncated response now raises instead of failing silently. Every run in this report was produced under the fix; only three runs across the sweep had ever hit the cap, and all were re-run. This reaches past this report: every prior VulcanBench result ran under the 16 K cap, and any run that hit it was scored as a wrong answer. Reports 07 and 08 cannot be re-checked — their raw runs have rotated out of retention.

Method & caveats

VulcanBench v3: 23 tasks from real merged post-cutoff PRs, network-isolated Docker sandboxes, deterministic hidden-test grading, one attempt per task per configuration. Opus 5 at $5/$25 per million tokens; reported high column is batch suite-bb35bfc8. Caveats: single attempts, and four of high’s five failures are wall-clock cutoffs — its ceiling here is partly the budget, not the model.
