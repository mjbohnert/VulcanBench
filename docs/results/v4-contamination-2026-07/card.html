Does contamination move Claude Opus 5’s score? (a controlled A/B)

Two 13-task arms, identical settings, differing only in whether the upstream fix merged before or after the model’s May 2026 cutoff
No detectable contamination effect. Genuine capability misses are 1 versus 1; the visible 11/13–vs–12/13 gap is a single task, inside the noise floor at n=13, and tracks repo-scale composition rather than memorisation. Before four harness defects were fixed, the same comparison read 7/13 vs 12/13 — a large gap pointing the wrong way.

1. No effect detected. One genuine miss per arm. A 7.7 pp headline gap is one task at n=13 — noise, not signal.

2. The gap is repo scale, not memory. The contaminated arm carries 11/13 large-or-xlarge repos vs 8/13, which is also why it cost 3.6× more and ran 2× longer per task. Report scale mix beside any cross-suite score.

3. Evidence against a large effect, not proof of none. At 11–12 of 13 the model is near ceiling, and a ceiling leaves memorisation little room to show itself.

Method. Both arms graded by deterministic hidden tests in network-isolated Docker sandboxes; every task pre-validated (gold patch = 1.0, unpatched = 0.0, deterministic over 3 runs). Contaminated arm: 13 VulcanBench v3 tasks whose upstream PRs merged 2026-02-12 to 2026-05-21. Clean arm: 13 tasks built for v4 from PRs merged 2026-06-10 to 2026-07-19. Surviving misses are the frontier-hard tail both suites deliberately contain (sqlglot canonicalization, regex leftmost-suffix, networkx Leiden). Caveats: amd64 images on an arm64 host (~2× emulation overhead inflates time and $/solved, not scores); one attempt per task, no pass@k; a single model. Results published before the harness fixes — Reports No. 4 and No. 08 — used different scoring semantics and are not comparable. Total spend $20.94.
